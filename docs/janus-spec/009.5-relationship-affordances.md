## 1. Core Philosophy: Relationship Affordances
Just as objects expose affordances (`003.5-affordance-model.md`), **relationships expose affordances**.
- An **Object Affordance** defines what a single entity offers (e.g. FVG offers `Entry`).
- A **Relationship Affordance** defines what the *connection* between two entities offers to a trade setup (e.g. an `OrderBlock` `CONTAINS` a `LiquidityPool` $\rightarrow$ this relationship offers `ConfidenceBoost`).

By querying relationship affordances, the Strategy System can evaluate complex setup topologies generically, without hardcoding combinations of object types.

---

## 2. Standard Relationship Affordances

### 2.1 `ConfidenceBoost` Affordance
Signifies that the connection between two objects reinforces their structural validity.
- **Physical Requirements**: Relationship: `CONTAINS`, `OVERLAPS`, or `ALIGNS`.
- **System Actions**: The Strategy System increases the confidence score of the setup, triggering a larger position size scaling.
- **Example**: `OrderBlock` overlapping with `VolumeShelf`.

### 2.2 `AbortSignal` Affordance
Signifies that the connection represents a fatal invalidation of the trade thesis.
- **Physical Requirements**: Relationship: `INVALIDATES` or `SWEEPS` (opposite direction).
- **System Actions**: The Policy Engine instantly halts entry, or the Execution System exits the position.
- **Example**: Price wicking through a `SwingHigh` in a LONG setup (sweeps target early, structure fails).

### 2.3 `ExecutionTrigger` Affordance
Signifies that the formation of this relationship is the exact trigger for entry execution.
- **Physical Requirements**: Relationship: `TRIGGERS` or `SWEEPS` (aligned direction).
- **System Actions**: The Strategy System generates a `Signal` and the Execution System instantiates the `TradeWorkflow`.
- **Example**: `LiquiditySweep` triggering a `BOS`.

---

## 3. Setup Composition Using Relationship Affordances

Strategies compose setups by joining object and relationship affordances:

```yaml
setup:
  # 1. Require an Entry object and a Target object
  nodes:
    - id: entryNode
      affordance: Entry
    - id: targetNode
      affordance: Target
  
  # 2. Require a specific relationship affordance between them
  edges:
    - source: entryNode
      target: targetNode
      affordance: ConfidenceBoost # The entry overlaps with our target zone or vice versa
```

This decouples the strategy entirely from concrete implementations. The engine queries for the *topology* of affordances rather than specific FVG/OB logic.
